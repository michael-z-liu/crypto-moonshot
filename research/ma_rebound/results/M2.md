# M2: does buying every MA touch pay, and are the major MAs special?

No model. Everything below is fills before 2025-01-01 (2020-2024), scenario `base` unless stated.
2025-26 outcomes are sealed: the holdout filter is applied when the event tables are read
(`read_cands`, `read_fills`, `read_labels` in `m2_baseline.py`) and nothing after the cut is
printed, plotted or written. An exit that runs into January 2025 from a 2024 fill is kept.

Run from `research/ma_rebound/`: `uv run --group research python m2_baseline.py` (about 50 seconds
end to end on this machine; the matched-control step is 8 processes, coin by coin, 17 seconds).
Intermediates are in `cache/m2/`. Tables (CSV, full):
[`t1_ma_entry_exit`](m2/t1_ma_entry_exit.csv) (15 MAs x 4 entries x 6 exits),
[`t2_timeframe_year`](m2/t2_timeframe_year.csv), [`t3_study_vs_placebo`](m2/t3_study_vs_placebo.csv),
[`t4_matched_controls`](m2/t4_matched_controls.csv), [`t5_majors`](m2/t5_majors.csv),
[`t6_timeframe_all_combos`](m2/t6_timeframe_all_combos.csv) (every timeframe, entry and exit).

## Definitions settled in M2

- **Trades.** One row per fill after dropping duplicates: orders of the same coin, MA, entry,
  scenario and fill candle count once, keeping the earliest trigger. No row was censored before
  2025 (asserted). bp = net x 10,000. `Net/u` = net return / the candidate's u. The stress column
  is the stress scenario's own fills (its fills are a subset of the base fills), same dedupe.
- **Fill rate** = distinct fills / candidates, over candidates whose order window (decision +
  T/2) ends by 2025-01-01. Candidates are counted with duplicates, so a refreshed order that
  triggered twice lowers the rate. Touch-market fills nearly always (rate 1.00). Trade counts use
  every fill before the cut, so a touch row can show a few more trades than candidates.
- **CI**: day bootstrap, 2,000 draws, seed 20261113, resampling the UTC days of the fills (days
  of the group; the union of both groups' days for the differences, so the two sides are resampled
  jointly). The overlapping trades of different MAs and coins on the same day are one cluster.
- **Years** in Table 2 are the **fill** year. The fill-rate column there is by trigger year.
- **Majors**: BTC, ETH and the 20 coins with the highest `liq` on the candidate's trigger day,
  ranked over all coins per UTC day from the 1d bars.
- **Matched random entries.** Pullback depth = (close / highest 5m close over the T ending at and
  including that candle) - 1, in units of u (the candidate's u, frozen at the trigger; a control
  uses its own day's `atr_d x sqrt(T)`). A control is a 5m candle of the same coin in the same UTC
  month as the candidate's trigger with `atr_d` defined, `liq` at or above $1M, depth within 0.25
  of the candidate's, and a close more than 0.5u (of that timeframe) from every one of the 15
  study MA levels (either side). It buys at the next 5m open, which must be before 2025; the exit
  is the close of the last 5m candle opening before entry + T/2, T and min(2T, 28 d), censored if
  it needs candles past a live series' end. Up to 3 controls per candidate, seeded (coin hash),
  all 244,589 + 300,949 candidates (no sampling needed). Returns are gross, from the same labels
  (E2, E1, E6) for the candidate. Each candidate's controls are averaged, then candidates are
  averaged (a pair is a candidate with at least one valid control). `u` columns divide each side
  by its own u. For 1M, min(2T, 28 d) = T, so "full" and "long" are identical. The pool size of
  random candidates was checked against a brute-force loop on 12 candidates (all equal).

## Go / no-go note (2020-24 only)

**Do the major MAs beat placebo MAs? No evidence that they do.** Pooled over all timeframes the
study minus placebo difference is +1.0 bp per trade (95% CI -4 to +6) for limit 0.05 E1, +1.0 (-5
to +7) for E3 and +1.3 (-3 to +6) for touch-market E1. Per timeframe, 1h is -0.1 (-3 to +2) and 4h
-6.6 (-18 to +4); 1d is +48 (-16 to +109); 1w and 1M have the study MAs below the placebos (-178
and -445 bp) but with intervals that span zero by a wide margin (1w placebo n = 2,023, 1M placebo
n = 54, so the 1M comparison is not meaningful). The net/u differences say the same. Odd lengths
(37, 73, 137, 163) behave like 50, 100 and 200.

**Do they beat matched random entries? Yes, gross, on 1h and 4h; it does not show that the MA is
the cause.** At T (limit 0.05) candidates beat their controls by +27 bp on 1h (95% CI +14 to +39,
181,762 pairs), +114 on 4h (+45 to +181, 40,227 pairs) and +57 over all timeframes (+39 to +74,
223,518 pairs); touch-market is similar (+25, +94, +42 over all). The controls earn about zero
on 1h (+2 bp), and negative on 4h and 1d. Two things limit the reading. First, a control need not
be above any average or in an uptrend, and a control that matches a deep pullback and is more than
0.5u from all 15 MAs is mostly below the MA band, while a candidate by construction held above its
MA for k bars. Second, the placebo MAs do as well as the study MAs, so what the entry captures is
"a pullback onto a 50-200 bar average in an up-move", not the major lengths. The limit entry also
buys at a resting price, which a next-open control does not. On 1d, 1w and 1M most candidates find
no control (1d 85% of candidates, 1w 96%, 1M 97%), leaving 1,369, 132 and 28 pairs (limit 0.05):
the 1d difference (+2,113 bp, CI +1,727 to +2,479, in a subset of candidates that does not
represent the rest) and everything on 1w and 1M are too thin to judge. The pairs also select
candidates that had a depth match, so their candidate means differ from Table 1.

**Is the mean net return per trade positive after costs? Only on the slower timeframes, and
mostly not distinguishable from zero.** Pooled study MAs, exit E1 (Table 6, Table 2):

| TF | limit 0.05 mean bp (95% CI) | touch-market mean bp (95% CI) | stress, limit 0.05 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1h | -0.4 (-14, +13) | -4.6 (-17, +8) | -25 |
| 4h | +49 (-9, +105) | +60 (+8, +114) | +25 |
| 1d | +366 (+143, +604) | +385 (+165, +620) | +343 |
| 1w | +197 (-144, +540) | +262 (-119, +628) | +178 |
| 1M | +237 (-329, +754) | +382 (-209, +864) | +202 |
| all | +26 (+4, +49) | +23 (+3, +43) | +3 |

- **By timeframe.** 1h is zero at best (E3, the take profit/stop pair, is -16.5 bp, CI -25 to
  -8; E4 and E5 are also negative). 4h is positive but its limit CI includes zero; E6 (2T
  hold) is the best exit on 4h (+74 limit, CI +6 to +142) and on all timeframes pooled (+39,
  +9 to +68). 1d is the one timeframe whose E1 interval excludes zero for both entries, and it
  survives the stress costs (+343 bp). 1w and 1M have means of +200 to +380 bp but intervals of
  about +/-350 and +/-550 bp: too thin to judge (1w 200 has 59 limit trades; 1M 20 has 343).
- **By entry.** A tighter buffer is better or equal: limit 0 > limit 0.05 > limit 0.15 on every
  timeframe for E1 (all: +30, +26, +18 bp); touch-market is similar to limit 0.05 (+23). The
  fill rate is about 0.5 for every limit buffer (duplicates counted as candidates), so about half
  of the setups never trade.
- **By exit.** E1 and E6 (plain time exits) are the best; E3 and E4 (take profit with stop) lose
  money on 1h; E3 roughly halves the mean on 4h to 1w, and E4 is negative on 4h (-8 bp) and
  pooled (-13 bp); E2 (T/2) is lower than E1 on the daily
  timeframe (+148 vs +366 bp). Medians are far below means on 1d and up (1d limit 0.05 E1: mean
  +366, median +96), so a few large winners carry the mean.
- **Majors cut.** The 22 majors do not look better than all coins: 1h -6 bp (-19 to +7), 4h +44
  (-3 to +96), 1d +249 (+5 to +481), all +19 (-6 to +43) for limit 0.05 E1 (touch-market all +16).

**Stable across years? No.** Every timeframe loses in 2022 and the winning years are mostly 2021 and
2023-24 (Table 2, exit E1, limit 0.05): 1h -39 bp in 2022 (-67 to -13) against +19 in 2021, +4 in 2023
and +8 in 2024 (CIs include zero); 4h +227 in 2021 (+93 to +365), -72 in 2022, +76 in 2023, -4 in
2024; 1d +802 in 2021, -570 in 2022 (-771 to -308, win rate 25%), +283 in 2023, +499 in 2024 (+98
to +926); 1w and 1M have the same sign pattern with CIs of +/-1,000 bp or more. All timeframes pooled (limit 0.05 E1, weighted by trades, so mostly 1h): 2020 +45 bp (-8 to +95), 2021 +105 (+50 to +163), 2022 -64 (-101 to -28), 2023 +31 (0 to +62), 2024 +32 (-19 to +84). The positive
averages on 4h and 1d therefore come from the bull years and are cut by the 2022 bear market;
this study has no buy-and-hold or market-neutral benchmark, so it cannot say how much of the 1d
mean is simply market direction (the controls above are the only comparison, and they differ by
the trend condition).

**Summary.** Buying every touch of a major SMA does not pay on 1h after costs, is marginal on 4h,
and is positive on 1d, mainly in 2021 and 2024 and not in 2022. The major lengths show no edge
over odd-length placebo averages. Candidates do beat random pullbacks on 1h and 4h gross, but the
controls lack the above-the-average condition. Weekly and monthly results are too thin to judge.
These are descriptions, not a recommendation: later milestones choose by the pre-registered rule.
