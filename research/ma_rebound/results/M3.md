# M3: the book and the scorecard

This milestone builds the portfolio book and the scorecard that every later rule and model is
judged with, and puts the *every candidate* rows in the ledger. Everything shown here is 2022-24.
2025-26 is computed only into `results/sealed/` (gitignored), which nobody has opened.

## What was built

Run from `research/ma_rebound/` with `uv run --group research python <script>`.

| Script | What it does | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `book.py` (`--prepare`) | Builds `cache/m3/`: the study candidates with their attributes (majors flag, order window end), one label table per entry x scenario x exit (48 tables), the forward-filled daily close matrix and BTC's 5m closes. Holds the book itself. | 7 s to prepare |
| `scorecard.py` | `scorecard(name, rule, scores=None, *, stage, source, placebos=20, reveal=False)` returns a `Card`, upserts the ledger row and writes the sealed file. `score_seeds(...)` scores a list of seed tables (a row per seed) and returns the per-seed rank values and their spread. `show(card)` prints a card. | see below |
| `check_book.py` | 35 PASS/FAIL checks: hand-computed synthetic cases, causality, scorecard plumbing, parity with the engine (`--parity-coins 0` runs every coin). | 6 s (every coin: 25 s) |
| `m3_every.py` | The 7 *every candidate* rows; the cards are also in `m3_every.txt`. | 6 s |

**Speed.** The tables load once per process (0.1 s). One book takes 0.14-0.19 s (the
every-candidate book has 220k rows in 2022-24, of which 12.5k are taken). An every-candidate call
(base and stress books cut at 2025, the two full-range books for the sealed file, the statistics)
takes 0.7-0.9 s. A scored call adds 20 placebo books (0.05 s each with a 20% selection) and takes
about 3.5 s.

## The book

`book.py` replays the M1 labels, whose entries and exits were already resolved by the
production `resolve_exit`. It uses the production `Book`, `EqualSlots` sizing, the `MaxPositions`
and `OnePerSymbol` risk checks, and the production metrics. It does not reimplement any of them.

1. **Order.** Rows are processed by fill time, then score (highest first), then the earliest
   trigger, then symbol. Before each new fill time, every open position whose exit time is at or
   before it is closed, in exit-time order, as in the engine.
2. **Entry.** Stake = realised equity / slots, where realised equity = cash plus the cost basis of
   open positions. The fill is skipped if the slots are full (`slots_full`), the coin is already
   held (`symbol_held`), or the stake exceeds the cash (`cash`). Otherwise the entry spends
   `min(stake, cash)`. A slot is taken only at the fill: resting orders reserve nothing.
3. **Accounting** follows `SimulatedBroker`:
   - buy fill = raw entry x (1 + the scenario's entry costs), and quantity = stake / (buy fill x
     (1 + fee));
   - sell fill = raw exit x (1 - the scenario's exit costs), and proceeds = quantity x sell fill -
     fee, with fee = 0.1% of quantity x sell fill.
   - So P&L = stake x the label's net return. `book.legs` asserts that its recomputed net equals
     the label's `net` to 1e-12, and the parity shows the engine's numbers to the last bit.
4. **Equity curve.** One point per midnight UTC, from the first entry's day to the day after the
   last exit (the engine's grid). Each point is cash plus every open position's quantity x the
   coin's last close at or before that midnight, which is the previous day's daily-bar close,
   forward-filled over days without candles. At a midnight that is the entry time itself, the raw
   entry price is used, as the engine does. Yearly returns and the overall metrics come from
   `backtest.metrics`.
5. **Range.** One continuous book. Fills before 2022-01-01 are ignored. Yearly trade numbers are
   by fill year.
6. **Data end.** A `censored` label is a position whose exit lies past the last candle of a
   series that has not ended. The book closes it at the coin's last 5m close, at that candle's
   close time, reason `series_end`, with market exit costs. The engine does the same at its data
   end. A censored *fill* (an order still resting at the data end) is no trade. Neither exists
   before 2025 (M2 asserted it), so they affect only the sealed years.

## Parity with the production engine

The test takes touch-market candidates on the 1h MAs of 50 coins drawn at random (seed
20261113), 24 of them delisted, with triggers in 2022-2023: 13,168 candidates. Each is a signal
whose signal candle is the touch candle, so the entry is at the next 5m open. The test runs them
through `run_backtest` with these settings:
- `ParquetCandleSource(DATA_DIR, until=DATA_END)` and `use_fine=True`;
- `Costs(fee=0.001, slippage=0.001)` and `ExitPlan(max_hold=8h)`;
- `EqualSlots(10)` with `max_positions=10`, $10,000 and start/end 2022-01-01 / 2024-01-01.

The book runs on the same candidates from the touch / base / E1 labels.

| Skipped | slots_full 2,606, symbol_held 2,230, cash 1,760 | identical |
| Entry ref, entry price, exit time, exit price, stake, P&L, net return | | all 6,572 identical (largest difference 0.0) |

| 2022 / 2023: trades, return, bp | 3,141, -81.50%, -51.26 / 3,431, -26.79%, -7.88 | identical |

Exit reasons: 6,569 time exits and 3 `series_end` (delisted coins).

The same test over every coin with a 1h touch candidate in 2022-23 (386 coins, 105,728
candidates) is also identical: 14,413 trades, every field and all 732 equity points, with zero
difference. There is exactly one difference in the counts, and it is explained:
`EPSUSDT-1h-200-t-202205090355` touches on EPS's last 5m candle before its delisting. M1 marks
its entry `expired`, so it has no label, and the engine skips it as `no_entry_candle`. Neither
books it. No other label enters later than the engine's entry candle.

So the only differences between the book and the engine would come from the labels themselves.
The parity found no defect in the M1 labels and no tolerance was needed.

## Definitions settled in M3

1. **Rule** (`scorecard.Rule`, frozen) has these fields:
   - `family` (limit | touch) and `buffer` (0, 0.05, 0.15; ignored for touch);
   - `exit` (E1..E6) and `pct` (None = every candidate);
   - `tfs` and `lengths` (the MA subset), `majors` and `slots`.
   With scores and `pct=None`, the scorecard uses 0.80, the base threshold. To trade every
   scored candidate, pass `pct=0`.
2. **Ledger key**: the name plus `sha1(rule.describe() | source)[:12]`. The scores' own digest
   is a column (`scores_sha`) but is not part of the key, as in the previous study. A rerun of
   the same name and config replaces its row, under an `fcntl` lock on `results/ledger.lock`.
3. **Scores**: a `cid, score` frame. A cid that is not a study candidate is an error, and a
   missing or NaN score is never traded. A candidate is selected at its decision. Ties at the
   same fill time are broken by score (highest first), then the earliest trigger, then symbol.
4. **Majors** follow M2's definition: BTC, ETH, or one of the 20 highest `liq` across all coins
   on the trigger's UTC day.
5. **Fill rate**: distinct fills / selected candidates, by **decision** year rather than fill
   year. Only candidates decided from 2022-01-01 whose order window (decision + T/2; decision +
   5m for touch) ends by the cut count, so 2024's rate contains no 2025 fill. Orders of the same
   coin, MA and fill candle count once, as in M2, while every candidate stays in the denominator.
   So a selection that drops refreshed duplicates shows a higher fill rate: random 20% selections
   showed 0.56-0.59 against 0.49-0.51 for every candidate.
6. **The 2022-24 card** comes from books cut at 2025-01-01 (fills before it). Their curves are
   used only up to 2025-01-01 00:00, so 2024's return is equity at that point over equity at
   2024-01-01. A 2024 fill that exits in 2025 counts with its label's return (the holdout is by
   fill time). The "2022-24" row pools the trades. Its book return is equity at 2025-01-01 over
   $10,000, and its drawdown is over the whole cut curve. The full-range books (base and stress)
   run only on the sealing path. `check_book.py` checks that a book cut earlier is an exact
   prefix of a longer one, so cutting changes nothing before the cut.
7. **Yearly max drawdown**: within the calendar year, with the equity at the year's start as the
   first peak.
8. **Mean hold**: exit minus entry, in hours.
9. **vs BTC**: the trade's raw exit / raw entry - 1, minus BTCUSDT's return from its last 5m close
   at or before the entry to its last 5m close at or before the exit. It is a mean over trades,
   in bp, and is descriptive only.
10. **Stress**: the same selection, on the `stress` labels, in its own book.
11. **Placebo**: the scores are permuted among the eligible scored candidates decided before 2025
    (the rule's family, MA subset and majors filter, before the threshold), within UTC month x
    timeframe of the decision. Draw j uses seed 1000 + j, keeps the same threshold and rule, and
    books 2022-24 only. The ledger keeps the draws' mean and 95th percentile of the 2022-24 mean
    bp.
12. **Bootstrap**: within each year, fill days are resampled with replacement. The statistic is
    the mean of the three yearly bp (sum of net / number of trades over the drawn days). There
    are 2,000 draws with seed 0, and the CI is the 2.5 and 97.5 percentiles.
13. **Without the 5 best days**: drop every trade of the 5 fill days in 2022-24 with the largest
    summed net return, then take the mean of the three yearly bp.
14. **Fragile**: some year's mean bp is below 0 without that year's single best trade. A year
    that is already negative counts as fragile too (see the points below).
15. **Verdict**: at least 100 trades every year, mean bp > 0 every year, book return > 0 every
    year, and stress mean bp (the mean of the yearly values) > 0. Scored variants also need a
    mean bp above the placebo's p95. For *every candidate* rows the placebo criterion is `n/a`
    and the verdict is judged on the other four. **Rank value** = the mean of the three yearly
    book returns, with the worst year's return as tie-break (column `worst_ret`).

Every row fails. Each has enough trades, but no row has positive mean bp in all three years,
every stress mean is negative, and every row is fragile. The best rank value is E6's -27.6% a
year, which is the bar a model's rule has to clear.

**The book is much worse than M2's per-trade means.** M2's deduplicated trades for limit 0.05 / E1
average -64 / +31 / +32 bp in 2022 / 2023 / 2024. The book's trades average -50 / -5 / -2 bp. Two
things select different trades:
- **Capacity.** With 10 slots, the book takes the first fills after a slot frees, and the setups
  cluster: on a market-wide drop many coins touch their MAs together. With effectively unlimited
  slots the book gives -62 / +21 / +13 bp over 30-41k trades a year. M2's per-day means are
  higher still, because a crowded rebound day counts once per MA there.
- **One position per coin.** That same unlimited book skips 118k fills as `symbol_held`. A coin
  held on one MA cannot take its other MAs' setups, while M2 counts every MA.

The 1h setups dominate because they are the most frequent and the shortest. The 1d setups, which
M2 found best, make up under 2% of the book's trades (227 of 12,509 for E1).

## Points for the master

- **The cash skip is common.** In the base-rule book, 109k fills are skipped for `cash` against
  89k for full slots. This is the production `EqualSlots` behaviour, and the parity confirms the
  engine skips the same fills. After any loss, realised equity / 10 exceeds the cash left when 9
  positions are open (each was sized on the higher equity), so the tenth slot stays empty until
  a position closes. The protocol says to skip a fill without enough cash, so the book does.
  But in a losing period the book effectively runs 9 slots. `CashCapped` is the production
  alternative, if the protocol should change.
- **"Fragile" wording.** The brief says a year's mean bp "turns negative" without its best trade.
  I followed the previous study's code: any year below 0 without its best trade is fragile, even
  one that was already negative. It makes no practical difference: "beats the baseline" needs a
  qualifying variant, which has every year positive, and then both readings agree.
- **The ledger key excludes the score values**, as in the previous study. A retrained model under
  the same name and `source` description replaces its row. M4 should put something that
  identifies the run (e.g. a run id) in `source`.
- **Fill rate is by decision year**, while every other yearly number is by fill year. The
  denominator excludes 2024 candidates whose order window crosses into 2025.
- **Duplicates.** Refreshed orders (one MA triggering several times before the touch) appear in
  the fill rate's denominator but fill once. The book takes the earliest trigger: the tie-break
  after score.
